How to play Turkish mangala

Mangala is the Turkish member of the mancala family. This page follows the four core rules of the Turkish federation (TAZOF) tournament text, with a small board diagram for each rule so you can check your own moves against it.

1 The board and setup

Each player has 6 small pits in front of them and one treasure on their right: 12 pits and 2 treasures in all. Every pit starts with 4 stones, so there are 48 stones in the game. Treasures start empty.

In the diagrams below you are the bottom row. Stones travel counter-clockwise: left to right along your row, into your treasure, then right to left along your opponent's row. The top-left box is your opponent's treasure and the bottom-right box is yours. Every pit of yours has an opponent pit directly across from it.

In tournaments the first player is drawn by lot; a coin toss does the same job at home.

2 A move: the first stone goes back

On your turn you pick one of your own pits and take all its stones. You drop the first stone back into the pit you took it from, then sow the rest one per pit, counter-clockwise. You pass through your opponent's pits as well, but you never drop a stone in the opponent's treasure; you skip it and keep going. Your own treasure does receive a stone.

4 The even rule: make an opponent pit even

If your last stone lands in a pit on the opponent's side and brings the pit to an even number (2, 4, 6, 8, and so on), you take every stone in that pit into your treasure. If the count is odd, nothing happens.

6 How a set ends and who wins

A set ends as soon as all six pits of either player are empty. The player who ran out first takes every stone left on the other side into their own treasure. Then the treasures are compared and the larger one wins. With 48 stones, 24-24 is a draw.

One detail: if your move captures the opponent's last stones and empties their side, it is the opponent who ran out, so the stones still on your side go to the opponent's treasure. The rule text says "the player whose own stones run out first", not "the player who moved".

Tournament match: three sets

In the tournament text a match is played over 3 sets; winning 2 sets wins the match (1 point for the winner, 0 for the loser). If the sets end level the match is a draw and each side gets 0.5; there is no extra set. The player who started second in set one starts set two, and the player who started set one starts set three.

Three habits worth building early

  1. Before every move, check whether any pit lands exactly in your treasure. An extra turn is a cheap tempo.
  2. Watch your opponent's pits holding an odd number of stones (1, 3, 5): those are the ones your last stone can turn even. Your opponent is looking at yours, so count your own pits too.
  3. An empty pit across from a crowded pit is a weapon. Keep such holes open and do not let your opponent plug them with a single stone.

Frequently asked questions

How many stones and pits are there in mangala?

12 pits with 4 stones each, so 48 stones, plus one treasure per player.

Why does the first stone go back into the same pit?

It is a core Turkish rule: you drop the first stone back where you picked up, then sow the rest to the right. A pit with many stones can therefore lap the whole board.

Do I put stones in my opponent's treasure?

No. The opponent's treasure is skipped while sowing; your own treasure does receive a stone.

Can a mangala game end in a draw?

Yes. A set that finishes 24-24 is a draw. In the tournament format, a match whose set wins are level after three sets is also a draw, worth 0.5 points each.

Which rule text is this?

This page follows the Turkish Mind and Intelligence Games Federation (TAZOF) tournament rules and the school tournament specification with the same wording. Regional variants of mangala exist, so agree on the rules first if you play by local custom.
